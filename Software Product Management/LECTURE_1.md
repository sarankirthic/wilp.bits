# 🎯 3. What Is Software Product Management?

The lecture gives a core definition:

> **Product Management is the process of identifying customer problems, deciding what product to build, guiding its development, and continuously improving it to create value for customers and the business.**

Break it down:

### Product Manager must:

1. Identify customer problems.
2. Decide **what to build**.
3. Guide development.
4. Continuously improve the product.
5. Create value for:

   * Customers
   * Business

---

# ⭐ 4. The Four Big Questions of Product Management

Product management must answer four fundamental questions:

```text
1. WHO are we building for?
2. WHAT should we build?
3. WHY should we build it?
4. HOW do we know it is successful?
```

These four questions are **very important**.

# 🧠 5. What Is the Job of a Product Manager?

The simplest explanation from the lecture:

> **Build the right product for the right customer.**

More specifically:

```text
Find the right problem
        ↓
For the right customer
        ↓
Build the right solution
        ↓
Create business + customer value
```

A product manager decides things such as:

* Which problem should be solved?
* Which feature should be built?
* Which feature should wait?
* What should go into the next release?
* What will generate the most customer value?
* What will have the biggest business impact?

# 🍔 6. Swiggy Example — Product Prioritization

The instructor used a hypothetical Swiggy-like example.

Customers complain:

* Add more vegetarian dishes.
* Mobile app is slow.
* Food delivery takes too long.
* Introduce loyalty points.
* Some areas aren't covered.

Imagine the company has:

* Limited developers
* Limited budget
* Limited time

You **cannot solve everything simultaneously**.

### So what do you prioritize?

The instructor emphasized that a slow app affects a very large portion of customers.

If the app is slow:

```text
Slow App
 ↓
Customer frustration
 ↓
Customers cannot order easily
 ↓
Customers may move to competitors
 ↓
Business impact
```

Food delivery delay is also highly important because **delivery is central to the business model**.

Meanwhile, something like adding more dishes may satisfy only a smaller subset of customers.

### Product Management = Prioritization

You must ask:

> **Which problem creates the greatest value/impact if solved now?**

```text

Can't build all 100

Analyze
```

**Product management is NOT simply collecting customer requests and building everything customers ask for.**

You have to determine:

* How many customers have the problem?
* How serious is it?
* What business impact does it have?
* Will solving it help other customers?

# 💰 21. TAM & SAM

The lecture introduced market sizing.

## TAM — Total Addressable Market

The **total potential market** for your product/service.

Example:

```text
5,000 restaurants
```

might theoretically be relevant.

But that doesn't mean all 5,000 will buy.

---

## SAM — Serviceable/Service Addressable Market

The portion of the total market that you can realistically serve/target.

Example from the lecture:

```text
TAM = 5,000 restaurants

↓ narrow based on actual target

SAM = 1,000 restaurants

↓ further realistic buyers

Potential immediate customers = ~500
```

### Core difference

```text
TAM
= Entire potential market

SAM
= Portion of market your offering can actually target/serve
```

---

# 📊 29. Measure Outcomes, Not Outputs

🔥 **Very important lecture point.**

A team might say:

> "We successfully shipped the feature."

That is an **output**.

But the real question is:

> **Did the feature actually solve the customer's problem?**

That's the **outcome**.

### Example:

Robot feature shipped:

❌ Output:

```text
Robot successfully deployed
```

✅ Outcome:

```text
Food delivery time
30 min → 10 min
```

That outcome tells you whether the solution actually created value.

# 👑 36. Product Manager vs Product Owner

The instructor made an important distinction.

### Product Manager

The PM:

* Sets product vision
* Prioritizes features
* Makes strategic decisions
* Reviews product direction
* Determines what should go into releases

### Product Owner

The Product Owner assists the PM by handling more detailed execution work such as:

* User stories
* Scenarios
* Documentation
* Detailed requirements

### Simple version

```text
PRODUCT MANAGER
       ↓
"What should we build and why?"
       ↓
Vision + Strategy + Prioritization

PRODUCT OWNER
       ↓
"How do we translate this into actionable work?"
       ↓
User stories + scenarios + details
```

The instructor emphasized that the Product Owner does **not** typically own the overall product vision or strategic prioritization in the same way the Product Manager does.
